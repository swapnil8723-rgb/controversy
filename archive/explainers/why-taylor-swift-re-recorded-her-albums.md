## What a "master" is

A **master** is the original recording of a song. Whoever owns it controls how that recording is licensed, for films, ads or streaming, and earns from it. Writing the song is a separate right: a songwriter can control the composition without owning the recording.

## Why she re-recorded

Swift's first six albums were recorded under her deal with Big Machine, which owned the masters. When Big Machine was sold in 2019 to a company owned by Scooter Braun, she objected publicly and said she would re-record those albums. New recordings are new masters, so the **"Taylor's Version"** albums were recordings she owned.

## What changed in 2025

On 30 May 2025 Swift said she had bought the original masters of her first six albums from Shamrock, the investment firm that had bought them in 2020. She now owns the recordings of all her albums. [Full timeline](/entertainment/controversies/taylor-swift-masters-dispute/).

## Why it matters beyond Swift

Her fight made master ownership a talking point well beyond her fans. In October 2025 NPR examined whether more artists are now getting ownership of their masters in their record deals.
