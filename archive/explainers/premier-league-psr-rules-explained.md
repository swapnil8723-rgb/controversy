## The rule in one line

Over a rolling three-season period, a club's losses, calculated the way the rules say, must not go above the permitted limit. In Everton's case the limit was £105 million.

## How a case works

1. The league checks each club's figures. If it believes a club is over the limit, it refers the case to an **independent commission**.
2. The commission decides whether there was a breach and how large it was, then sets a sanction. Points deductions are one option.
3. Either side can appeal to an **appeal board**, which can change the sanction.

## Everton as the worked example

- **Period to 2021/22:** the commission found a loss of £124.5 million against the £105 million limit and deducted 10 points in November 2023. On appeal this became six.
- **Period to 2022/23:** Everton admitted exceeding the limit by £16.6 million. A second commission deducted two points in April 2024, reducing the penalty partly because the two periods overlapped.

Full dates and sources are on the [Everton points deductions record](/football/controversies/everton-psr-points-deductions/).

## Is Man City's case a PSR case?

Partly. One of the six groups of charges against Manchester City concerns the league's profitability and sustainability rules for 2015/16 to 2017/18, but most concern financial information, contracts, UEFA rules and co-operation.
