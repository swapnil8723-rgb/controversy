## Laws vs spirit

The **Laws** are the rulebook. Umpires apply them, and a dismissal under the Laws stands. The **spirit of cricket** is MCC's statement of how the game should be played: respect for opponents, umpires and the game. It is not something umpires use to overturn a legal dismissal. That is why these rows are about sportsmanship, not rules.

## Three cases, side by side

| Case | What happened | Legal? | Why people argued |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Bairstow, Lord's 2023](/cricket/controversies/jonny-bairstow-stumping-lords-2023/) | Left his crease after ducking a short ball; stumped | Yes: third umpire ruled the ball was not dead | He was not trying to run; England said they would not win that way |
| [Dean, Lord's 2022](/cricket/controversies/deepti-sharma-charlie-dean-run-out/) | Backing up too far at the non-striker's end; run out | Yes: MCC said it was properly officiated | The old "Unfair Play" label, and a dispute over whether she was warned |
| [Mathews, Delhi 2023](/cricket/controversies/angelo-mathews-timed-out/) | Not ready to face within two minutes; timed out | Yes: World Cup playing conditions, backed by MCC | His helmet strap broke; no one had been timed out in international cricket before |

## What changed

MCC moved the non-striker run-out out of the Unfair Play law and into the Run out law from 1 October 2022, and said non-strikers should stay in their ground until they see the ball leave the bowler's hand. In other words, the Laws now treat it as an ordinary run-out.
